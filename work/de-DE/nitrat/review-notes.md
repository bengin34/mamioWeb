## Two places where the article deliberately refuses the popular answer

1. **"Never reheat baby puree" is not what the source says.** The BfR sentences are: store
   nitrate-rich foods chilled; prepared vegetables, especially spinach, should not be kept warm for
   longer periods; cool leftovers quickly and keep them refrigerated until eaten [1]. That is about
   holding warm and about storage. The article quotes it and then says explicitly that a general
   ban on reheating is not in the source. German parenting sites commonly state the ban; if the
   reviewer knows a Tier-1 document that does say it, it should be added rather than inferred.

2. **Rote Bete is handled as a negative finding.** Parents ask about beetroot constantly, and it is
   not in the BfR list of nitrate-rich foods that was opened (leafy vegetables, radishes, fresh
   culinary herbs, kohlrabi) [1]. The article says the list does not name it and that no separate
   statement is available from these sources, instead of silently including or excluding it. Worth
   a second opinion: a different BfR document may cover beetroot specifically.

## Age framing
The quantified risk sentence is the source's own and is about **1 to 3 year olds** eating more than
one high-nitrate spinach meal in a day [1] — not about the first year. The article carries that age
range verbatim. Separately the source says infants in the first months of life are the most
vulnerable group but are normally still milk-fed [1]. Both are in the text, kept apart.

## Mechanism
Nitrite → methaemoglobin → reduced oxygen transport → hypoxia, with the symptom sequence (fatigue
and headache first, cyanosis at higher methaemoglobin fractions) all quoted from [1]. The
"blue baby" phrasing was not used because the opened page does not use it.

## Dropped
Any mg/kg figure, including a limit for baby food (EU 2023/915 is cited for leafy vegetables only) ·
"spinach from month X" (no source gives an age) · a ranking of low-nitrate vegetables (the source
gives examples, not a ranking) · the reheating ban.
